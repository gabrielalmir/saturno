As a manager, what interests me in the project development view is from the story card up (i.e., stories and epics).
As an analyst, what often interests me is from the storyboard view down.

As a manager, or sprint organizer, planning is done based on L2 (problems/change requests - both require planned development).
As an analyst, there are often L1s to handle that I need to justify to my manager when the L2 is delayed; in my company's reality, an analyst works with a percentage of L1/L2 (the higher the position, the higher the amount of L2 tends to be) - however, this also needs to be adaptive depending on the volume of tickets I have.

As a manager, Trello can be poor for macro visualization.
As an analyst, Trello is very good (especially with power-ups) combined with Excel metrics, but tools like OpenProject and Azure DevOps can be too bureaucratic for a small team within a large department to maintain.

As a manager, I want a tool that shows me the macro functioning of my teams, helps me align goals with other departments, plan future expenses due to future developments, and plan for unexpected situations.
As an analyst, I would like a tool that helps me day-to-day, that can help me plan tasks without taking up too much of my working time, and that makes it possible to communicate to management what I am working on.

Today, at the company where I work, we have JIRA as part of our workflow, but we don't have the version with sprints; it acts as an approved intake channel. But we constantly need to keep updating spreadsheets and Trello.

As a manager, there are plans I don't want to worry my analysts with; they are not yet ready to be discussed.
As an analyst, I would like to know what is being planned for the next few sprints; what interests me is usually the short term and at most the medium term.

Even if the sprint has been defined, it must be flexible so that new pending items, such as urgent developments, can be aligned or substituted.

As an analyst, I care about defining software quality improvement metrics, such as time we can productively set aside to work on issues that don't have an immediate impact, such as: updating the programming language version, updating the framework, implementing Redis, optimizing queries, etc.
As a manager, I care about keeping company goals aligned with the department's perspective.

As a manager, I would like to be able to create multiple organizations that define different teams for which I am responsible.
As an analyst, I would like to be able to be invited to multiple teams in which I participate or have actions.

Unfinished cards should be able to be moved to the next sprint.
As a manager, I want to be able to consider non-working days, employee absences, and holidays.
I want to be able to evaluate planned hours based on the actual work capacity of the employee (assuming the L1/L2 percentage and predicted time available for work).

An epic has multiple story points, a story point has multiple tasks, and each task can be performed by one or more analysts.

As an analyst, I like the Trello / Todoist view, as it is comfortable to view and manage the cards I am working on in a kanban style.
As a manager, I would like to be able to view a more macro and faster way to understand what is being proposed.

As an analyst, my view is often based on the current sprint and at most the next one.
As a manager, my view is ideally based on whichever sprint I want to analyze.

As an analyst, the tool should ideally be easy to self-host.
As a manager, I want to have the security of having the tool in-house and being able to contract services if internal costs increase.

As both a manager and an analyst, I am very interested in the possibility of integrating with existing tools on the market and those that are already part of my work.
